There isn't any reliable dataset that tracks "bananas eaten per minute" by species, so any answer has to be a rough back-of-the-envelope estimate.

The only species for which we have reasonably good banana consumption data is humans:

Humans eat roughly 130–150 billion bananas per year worldwide.
That works out to about 250,000–290,000 bananas per minute.
A reasonable midpoint is ≈270,000 bananas per minute.

For other species, estimates are much harder because bananas are usually only a small part of their diet and are often provided by humans rather than eaten in the wild.

Species/group	Very rough bananas eaten per minute worldwide
Humans	~270,000
Monkeys (all species combined)	Hundreds to a few thousand
Great apes (chimpanzees, gorillas, orangutans, bonobos)	Tens to hundreds
Fruit bats	Tens to hundreds
Pet parrots and other birds	Tens to hundreds
Elephants	Single digits to tens
Other animals (squirrels, raccoons, reptiles, insects, etc.)	Hundreds to a few thousand combined

The surprising result is that humans almost certainly account for well over 99% of all cultivated bananas eaten on Earth. Even if every non-human animal that occasionally eats bananas were combined, they would likely still consume far fewer bananas than humans do.

So a reasonable global estimate would be:

Total bananas eaten every minute on Earth: roughly 270,000–300,000.

Humans: ~270,000
All other species combined: perhaps a few thousand to a few tens of thousands at most.

Because the animal numbers are so uncertain, the exact total could easily be off by a factor of several, but humans dominate banana consumption by an enormous margin.






It varies a lot by size, diet, and whether bananas are a main food or just a treat. For most animals, bananas are not their primary diet, so these are rough daily amounts if bananas are part of what they eat:

Primates
Monkeys (e.g., macaques): about 1–3 bananas per day (often only part of their diet).

Chimpanzees: about 2–5 bananas per day.

Gorillas: about 2–6 bananas per day, but they mostly eat leaves and stems.

Large animals
Elephants: about 20–50 bananas per day if available, but they usually eat hundreds of kilograms of other plants.

Bats
Fruit bats: about 1–3 bananas (or equivalent fruit) per night.

Birds
Parrots: about 0.25–1 banana per day (usually mixed with seeds/fruits).

Toucans: about 0.5–1 banana per day.

Rodents
Squirrels: a few slices, about 0.1–0.3 of a banana per day.

Rats/mice: small pieces, about 0.05–0.1 banana per day.

Farm animals
Horses: 1–3 bananas per day as treats.

Cows: 2–6 bananas per day (often with peels in some regions).

Pigs: 2–5 bananas per day.

Pets
Dogs: small dogs 0.1–0.5 banana; large dogs up to 1 banana per day.

Cats: a few bites at most (they don’t usually prefer fruit).

Insects
Fruit flies: tiny amounts; one banana can feed hundreds per day.


Getting a “total population” for each animal that eats bananas is tricky because many of these are broad groups (like “monkeys” or “birds”) with dozens or thousands of species. Still, here are very rough global estimates to give you a sense of scale:

Primates
Monkeys (all species combined): roughly 100 million–200 million.

Chimpanzees: about 170,000–300,000.

Gorillas: about 300,000+ (mostly western gorillas).

Baboons: about 500,000–1 million.

Large animals
Elephants (all species): about 400,000–500,000 total.

Bats
Fruit bats (flying foxes and relatives): millions to tens of millions globally (varies widely by species).

Birds
Parrots (all species): tens of millions, but many species are endangered.

Toucans: a few million combined across species.

Rodents
Squirrels: hundreds of millions globally.

Rats and mice: billions (they are among the most numerous mammals on Earth).

Farm animals (domesticated)
Cows: about 1.5 billion worldwide.

Pigs: about 1 billion.

Horses: about 60 million.

Pets
Dogs: about 900 million–1 billion (including strays).

Cats: about 600–800 million.

Insects
Fruit flies: impossible to count precisely, but populations are in the trillions globally.
